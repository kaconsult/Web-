Trusts and asset protection

A trust can separate family wealth from business risk, plan succession across several countries and keep assets under professional management. We help you choose the jurisdiction and introduce you to a licensed trustee that sets up and runs the trust.

Who it is for

  • Entrepreneurs who want to separate family assets from business risk
  • Families with members and assets in several countries who need a clear succession plan
  • Investors from the Caucasus and Central Asia with assets abroad who want professional, independent oversight

How a trust works

The settlor transfers assets to a trustee, which holds and manages them for the beneficiaries under a trust deed and the law the deed chooses. A protector can be appointed to oversee the trustee. Once the transfer is made, the assets no longer belong to the settlor, and that is what gives a properly structured trust its protective effect.

Jurisdictions we work with

  • Cook Islands. A purpose-built asset protection regime under the International Trusts Act 1984. According to local trustees, Cook Islands courts do not recognise foreign judgments based on law inconsistent with the Act, creditor claims face short time limits, and only the names of the trust and the trustee are filed with the registrar.
  • Nevis and Belize. Caribbean jurisdictions with dedicated international trust legislation. Belize’s Trusts Act, for example, provides that its courts will not set aside a Belize-law trust or recognise claims against it based on foreign law on marriage, succession or insolvency.
  • Cyprus. An EU jurisdiction with an International Trusts Law (1992, substantially amended in 2012). Cyprus has also ratified the Hague Trust Convention. Often used for succession planning and holding structures with substance in the EU.
  • Luxembourg. A civil-law financial centre that recognises foreign trusts as a party to the Hague Trust Convention. Suited to families who want custody and fiduciary services under EU regulation.

How we work

  1. First call: your goals, family, assets and tax residences, under an NDA.
  2. Jurisdiction and structure: we compare the options and coordinate with your tax and legal advisers.
  3. Introduction: we introduce you to a licensed trustee or bank, which carries out its own KYC and source-of-funds checks.
  4. Set-up and ongoing support: the trustee drafts the deed, opens the accounts and administers the trust; we remain your point of contact.

What a trust cannot do

A trust is not a way to avoid existing debts or tax. Transfers made to defeat known creditors can be challenged, and courts look closely at timing and intent. Trusts are also generally covered by tax reporting and international information exchange. Every structure needs advice on the tax rules of the countries where the settlor and beneficiaries live, and we work only with clients whose funds have a clear, documented source.

Frequently asked questions

Who owns the assets in a trust?

The trustee holds legal title and manages the assets for the beneficiaries under the trust deed. The settlor no longer owns them, although a protector or a letter of wishes can guide how the trustee acts.

Will a trust protect assets from existing creditors?

No. A trust is a tool for planning ahead. Transfers made when a claim already exists or is expected can be challenged.

Does K+A act as trustee or hold client funds?

No. We advise and introduce. The trust is set up and run by a licensed trustee, and client money never passes through our accounts.

Which jurisdiction is best?

It depends on where the family lives, what the assets are and what the trust should achieve. The Cook Islands and Nevis focus on asset protection; Cyprus and Luxembourg suit families who want an EU-based structure.
